5 · Where the day gets heavy

These are the places your answers kept circling back to. Each one keeps what you said separate from what I'm inferring.

A. The nights feed the days

YOU SAID 3 to 7 wake-ups a night; rated 4.5–5. Moving her to her own bed hasn't happened because "we're just too tired to even deal with nighttime battles on top of daytime battles."
MY INFERENCE Every other part of the day starts from this level of tiredness.

B. 7:15–9:00: everything arrives at once

YOU SAID Work you can't easily leave, the baby, four kids arriving one at a time, checklist reminders, the breakfast question, and the tablet negotiation. Morning Basket gets lost.
MY INFERENCE Samyah's missed 6:00 wake-up removes breakfast from the morning, so the breakfast decision moves onto you at your busiest moment.

C. You are the household's clock

YOU SAID "It all depends on me reminding them." You are Samyah's alarm, the reminder for every task the middles do, and the reason the teens stay on schedule. The written check-ins don't happen.
MY INFERENCE The schedules and checklists exist and the kids are capable, so what seems to be missing is something besides you that starts each next step.

D. Working while supervising, all day

YOU SAID Nearly four early-morning hours ≈ one hour of focused work. The afternoon and evening are interrupted too (4.5), with work happening in the basement among the kids and the TV.
MY INFERENCE Your work and your parenting are almost never separated in time or space, so both feel unfinished.

E. Meals decided in the moment

YOU SAID Breakfast on the spot = 5; decided ahead = ~2–2.5. Lunch is fend-for-yourself; dinner is improvised about half the time; meals are eaten separately.

F. The 4:00 handoff and the evening

YOU SAID Your husband arrives exhausted. You hand off and go back to work until 7:30–8:00. Bedtime is "very hectic, not peaceful."
MY INFERENCE The handoff is between two depleted adults, and there's no shared stopping point that marks the end of the workday for either of you.

G. Unwritten logistics (what happened Friday)

YOU SAID Kids woken late, nothing prepared the night before, breakfast was yogurt, 3 hours of therapy with hungry kids, a DMV trip that failed because your license was at home, rushing to post a carousel, then late again for Samyah's 4:00 PT, where you had a panic attack.
