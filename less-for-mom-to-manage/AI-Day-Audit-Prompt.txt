I'm a mom who loves my family, and I'm trying to make less of our daily life depend entirely on me so I can be more present with my children.

I want you to interview me about my actual daily flow, using the same type of thoughtful questions a family-routines consultant would ask.

Your job right now is NOT to fix my schedule, suggest routines, or give generic productivity advice. Your job is to help me see the day I'm actually living.

STEP 1: HOW TO INTERVIEW ME
- Ask me ONE question at a time, starting with when I wake up and moving through the day until bedtime. Include what happens overnight.
- Ask follow-up questions when my answers are vague.
- Don't assume every day looks the same. Distinguish typical days from particularly difficult ones. Ask me to walk you through a recent hard day.
- When I give you a long answer, briefly reflect back what you heard so I can correct anything you misunderstood.
- Every so often, let me know roughly how many questions are left.
- If I upload or paste schedules, checklists, or calendars, treat them as the plan, and compare them with what I tell you actually happens.
- You don't need my children's names. I may refer to them by age.

STEP 2: WHAT TO HELP ME IDENTIFY
- What actually happens, including interruptions and unexpected needs
- What I'm physically doing
- What I'm mentally managing, remembering, deciding, and anticipating
- What my children can already do independently
- What responsibilities my husband or others already own
- Where the day consistently becomes overwhelming
- What I wish could happen but rarely does
- How much time and energy different responsibilities require (near the end, ask me to rate how draining each one feels from 1 to 5)

STEP 3: WHEN YOU HAVE ENOUGH INFORMATION
Organize my answers into:
1. A visual day map: a simple timeline or table I can read on my phone, showing my daily flow from wake-up to bedtime.
2. A report I can save or print that shows my daily flow, recurring decisions, interruptions, responsibilities, and pressure points.

STEP 4: GROUND RULES
- Separate what I actually told you from what you infer. Label each clearly.
- Flag missing information instead of inventing details.
- Do not recommend solutions yet. I want to examine the map and identify what's making my days heavy before deciding what needs to change.

Start by asking me your first question.
