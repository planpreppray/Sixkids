Before You Begin

Friend, let's see what's actually happening.

You love your family. But somewhere between the appointments, the meals, the homeschooling, the interruptions, and everyone needing something, it's easy to lose sight of just how much you're carrying.

And sometimes the hardest part isn't everything you're physically doing.

It's everything you're constantly thinking about.

That's why we're starting here.

Before we create another schedule, change another routine, or add another thing to our to-do lists, we're going to take an honest look at the days we're already living.

We're going to use AI to help us do some of that mental work. Not to tell us how to run our families, but to help us see what we might be missing.

How to use this resource
  1. Open the free version of ChatGPT or Claude.
  2. Copy the entire AI Day Audit prompt from the next pages.
  3. Paste it into a new conversation.
  4. Answer the questions one at a time. You can use voice dictation if that's easier.
  5. Take your time. You don't need to finish the interview in one sitting.
  6. Review the map AI creates and correct anything it misunderstood.

You aren't trying to fix your whole life today. You're simply paying attention to the life you're actually living.

A gentle privacy noteYou don't need to share your children's full names, birthdays, addresses, medical details, or anything else that identifies them. "My 9-year-old" works just as well. Share only what helps the AI understand your day.

I'm a mom who loves my family, and I'm trying to make less of our daily life depend entirely on me so I can be more present with my children. I want you to interview me about my actual daily flow, using the same type of thoughtful questions a family-routines consultant would ask. Your job right now is NOT to fix my schedule, suggest routines, or give generic productivity advice. Your job is to help me see the day I'm actually living. STEP 1: HOW TO INTERVIEW ME - Ask me ONE question at a time, starting with when I wake up and moving through the day until bedtime. Include what happens overnight. - Ask follow-up questions when my answers are vague. - Don't assume every day looks the same. Distinguish typical days from particularly difficult ones. Ask me to walk you through a recent hard day. - When I give you a long answer, briefly reflect back what you heard so I can correct anything you misunderstood. - Every so often, let me know roughly how many questions are left. - If I upload or paste schedules, checklists, or calendars, treat them as the plan, and compare them with what I tell you actually happens. - You don't need my children's names. I may refer to them by age. STEP 2: WHAT TO HELP ME IDENTIFY - What actually happens, including interruptions and unexpected needs - What I'm physically doing - What I'm mentally managing, remembering, deciding, and anticipating - What my children can already do independently - What responsibilities my husband or others already own - Where the day consistently becomes overwhelming - What I wish could happen but rarely does - How much time and energy different responsibilities require (near the end, ask me to rate how draining each one feels from 1 to 5) STEP 3: WHEN YOU HAVE ENOUGH INFORMATION Organize my answers into: 1. A visual day map: a simple timeline or table I can read on my phone, showing my daily flow from wake-up to bedtime. 2. A report I can save or print that shows my daily flow, recurring decisions, interruptions, responsibilities, and pressure points. STEP 4: GROUND RULES - Separate what I actually told you from what you infer. Label each clearly. - Flag missing information instead of inventing details. - Do not recommend solutions yet. I want to examine the map and identify what's making my days heavy before deciding what needs to change. Start by asking me your first question.

End of prompt
