# Wendy's Family Assessment Method: draft v0.1

**Purpose:** write down how Wendy actually helps a homeschool mom, precisely enough to (1) run a small paid pilot with Wendy reviewing every plan, and later (2) build a guided tool. The method comes first; the technology is chosen after the pilot.

## 1. The promise (to test, not assume)

> "Find out why one part of your day keeps falling apart, and leave with a plan that actually fits your house."

Why this wording:
- **"Not sure what would actually work"** is the #1 barrier in the Sept 2026 survey (29 of 56).
- **"I know what to do, I just can't stick with it"** is the most common self-description (22 of 56).

The product is a diagnosis plus a fitted plan, **not** more teaching.

**The outcome Wendy names:** "Margin, presence, joy: she's actually living and present in her life."

**Boundaries:**
- Peace comes from Christ, not from systems.
- This is not counseling or medical advice. Health, marriage or grief concerns get flagged to Wendy and referred out, never handled in the plan.

## 2a. The six-step process (Wendy's refined version, Oct 4)
1. **"Walk me through your day."** Listen for what actually happens: her energy, how much depends on her, what the children know to do, what help she has, and when the overwhelm starts. Notice what the family loves and wants to protect.
2. **Ask what she thinks the problem is.** Her assessment matters. What does she want to change? What would bring the most relief? Hold your own observations alongside hers.
3. **Find what's blocking that goal.** Look for the one change that would create breathing room. Sometimes that's the morning. Sometimes it's protecting quiet time, or meeting a toddler's needs before changing homeschool.
4. **Ask deeper questions to test possible solutions.** Check each idea against her children, space, support, convictions and past experience. When an answer rules something out, adjust the suggestion.
5. **Choose one thing to try together.** Explain how it could help, and agree on an approach she can realistically use. Let that first change create room before deciding what else needs attention.
6. **Follow up on what actually happened.** What feels easier, unchanged or harder? What does she notice now? Keep, adjust or replace the approach.

**The heart of the method:** help her understand what's making life heavy, find where relief would matter most, and work out a change that fits her particular family. The detailed notes in section 2 below support these six steps.

## 2. The assessment, in Wendy's order

### Step 1. Let her talk: what feels hard?
"I think I have them talk to me, and I ask guiding questions."
- Where does she think the overwhelm is coming from?
- What frustrates her during the day? When does she first notice it?
- **[WENDY]** Your 3–5 favorite opening questions, word for word.

### Step 2. Get the full picture of the family
"I have to see the full picture of their life."
- Children: number, ages, stages, special needs or executive-function challenges, babies and toddlers.
- Husband: work hours, involvement (bedtime, chores, projects), what she wishes he carried.
- Other support: older kids, grandparents, co-op, paid help.
- Fixed commitments: co-op, lessons, appointments, work, church. Mark the days that look different.
- Mom's own capacity: health, sleep, pregnancy or postpartum, energy curve through the day.
- **[WENDY]** Which of these do you ask about every time, and which only when something comes up?

### Step 3. What does "keeping up" mean to her?
- A deep-cleaned house, or reasonably tidy? Which rooms matter most to her, and to her husband?
- What would a "good enough day" look like, in her words?
- **[WENDY]** How do you tell when her expectations are the problem rather than her system?

### Step 4. Find where the day breaks down
Look at **meals, toddler routines, evenings and connection with her husband** first (these come up most). Then classify the breakdown using the four causes from the diagnostic:

| Cause | Signs she'll describe | Repair pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **A. No flexible version** | Every day is different; one change wrecks the day; a season of change (baby, move, illness) | Build the routine in three sizes (A = 3 steps or fewer, B, C). Pick the size each morning. |
| **B. Exceeds what the hour holds** | Finishes about half on a good day; can't decide what to cut | Time every step honestly, add it up, cut until it fits plus margin |
| **C. Runs entirely on mom** | "If I don't say it, it doesn't happen"; works only when she has energy | Get it out of her head: visible steps, photo of "done," point instead of tell |
| **D. Collapses at one hour** | "Mornings are fine, afternoons aren't"; can name the hour | Log 3 days at that window, find what repeats, change only that window |

**[WENDY]** What if two causes are equally strong? (The diagnostic says go with the one that makes her wince. Is that what you actually do?)

### Step 5. Show what could change (specific to this family)
Patterns from Wendy's own examples and the October consult:
- **Five children under five:** maintain only the living room, dining room and kitchen. Deep cleaning waits for a later season.
- **Capable older children:** they take over breakfast or lunch, or learn specific dinners.
- **Toddler chaos:** give the toddler a more intentional routine (busy bins rotated daily, a set quiet time).
- **Afternoon overstimulation:** a quiet hour in separate rooms, then a short reading block, then a **15-minute focused cleaning project** rather than a general "clean up."
- **Kids who can't follow "clean your room":** photograph each space when it's done and post it; the photo becomes the standard. Use short, specific tasks for kids with executive-function struggles.
- **Marriage feeling neglected:** one or two simple ways to connect, not a program.
- **Busy evenings:** start dinner earlier, or plan a quick or frozen meal for the known hard night.
- **[WENDY]** Add 5–10 more "if you see X, you usually suggest Y" rules from your own experience.

### Step 6. Choose changes that create room to live
- **One realistic change to test first** (priority one), with a first step she can take within 48 hours.
- At most 2–3 priorities in total, in order.
- **What she can set down for now**, using the Carry Question where it fits: "Did God actually ask me to carry this?"
- **[WENDY]** When do you tell her to simplify instead of adding a routine? What's the rule?

## 3. The cleaning plan logic (from the Sept 7 design session)
1. List every space: kitchen, bathroom(s), kids' bedrooms, master bedroom, living room, family room, dining room, laundry, entry or mudroom, office, homeschool room, playroom, basement, garage, car, front yard, backyard, plus any she adds.
2. Each space has tasks at four frequencies: **daily / weekly / monthly / seasonal**. Frequency belongs to the *task*, not the space.
3. Assign by **age and stage** (default suggestions she can adjust up or down for special needs or ability). A child can own a space's daily tasks while an adult covers the monthly ones.
4. Rhythm: **morning chores** (once-a-day jobs like feeding the dog and unloading the dishwasher), plus **two tidies a day** (afternoon and evening) covering each space's daily tasks.
5. Shrink it to her season (step 5 rules), then make it visible (photos, posted lists).
- **[WENDY]** Default task lists for each space, and age baselines (for example, what a 4, 7, 10 and 13-year-old can typically own).

## 5. What she receives
- **Her plan:** what's breaking and why (cause A–D, in her words) → priority one and its 48-hour first step → up to 2 more priorities → what to set down → her cleaning plan (and meal plan later) → one verse and encouragement written by Wendy, not generated.
- **A printable rhythm sheet** (order, not the clock) that fits the planner.

## 6. Support that does something (instead of an empty group)
- **A weekly 3-question check-in** (form or text reply):
  1. Did you try your one change? (Yes / partly / no)
  2. What happened, in one line?
  3. Keep it, shrink it, or swap it?
- **Wendy's reply:** adjust the plan from the answer. The 7-day voice-message check-in from the clarity-plan skill stays.
- **A community only if pilot families ask for one,** and only with a weekly activity built in.

## 7. Small paid pilot (measure the result and Wendy's time separately)
- **Who:** 5–10 families, invited first from the 29 survey respondents who said yes or maybe to a call.
- **Price:** **[WENDY]** decides. If the Bootcamp is included, it can't go below the $147 promise.
- **Flow:**
  1. Written intake (steps 1–4).
  2. Plan drafted with AI help.
  3. **Wendy reviews and edits every plan.**
  4. Delivered within 48 hours.
  5. Check-ins at day 7 and week 3.
- **Measure for each family:**
  1. Did she use the plan?
  2. Did the one change hold on at least 4 of 7 days by week 3?
  3. Where did she need a human?
  4. Would she pay again or recommend it?
  5. **Wendy's total minutes per family**, compared with about 4 hours for a full consultation.
- **Decide after the pilot:**
  - If plans are used and Wendy's time is under about 60–75 minutes per family → package and build.
  - If plans aren't used → fix the method before any technology.
